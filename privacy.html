Orca Colab Community

Privacy policy

Orca Colab Community · last updated 13 November 2026

Orca Colab Community ("the app") is an open-source plugin and command-line tool that runs entirely on your own computer. This policy explains what it does with your Google data.

Data the app accesses

When you sign in with Google, the app requests these permissions:

  • Email address and basic profile (userinfo.email, userinfo.profile): used only to label the signed-in account in the app, so you can tell several accounts apart.
  • Colab (auth/colaboratory): used to create, list and delete Colab runtimes in your account, to run commands on them, and to read your Colab compute-unit balance, each time you or a coding agent you run asks the app to do so.

Where your data is stored

Your Google access and refresh tokens, email address and name are stored only on your computer, in ~/.orca-colab/google-oauth.json, with file permissions that allow only your user account to read it. The app talks directly from your computer to Google's servers.

The project operates one small server, the sign-in relay (orca-colab-token-relay.fhq981014.workers.dev, a Cloudflare Worker). Google requires the app's client secret whenever a sign-in code is exchanged for tokens or a token is refreshed; so that the secret is not shipped inside the app, those two requests go through the relay, which adds the secret, forwards the request to Google and returns Google's answer unchanged. That is all it does. It does not store or log the codes and tokens that pass through it (request logs are disabled), never calls any Colab API and cannot act on your account by itself. To stop one client from using up its free quota, it counts requests per IP address (at most 30 a minute and 300 an hour); that count is kept in memory only and is discarded once the hour has passed. Its full source is public. Cloudflare, which runs it, sees the connection as for any website. You can avoid the relay entirely by using your own Google OAuth client (see the README).

Apart from that relay, the project collects no data. Nothing about you, your Google account or your Colab usage is sent to the developers or to any third party. The app contains no analytics or tracking.

If you use the app with a remote Orca server you control, the app sends your tokens, encrypted, to that server so it can act for you there. They are stored on that server in the same way and go nowhere else.

Sharing

The app does not sell, share or transfer your Google user data, and does not use it for advertising, for training AI models, or for any purpose other than the features described above.

The app's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing your data

  • Run orca-colab auth logout, or sign out in the Orca panel: the app revokes its token at Google and deletes it from your computer.
  • You can also withdraw the app's access at any time by removing "Orca Colab Community" at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Deleting the ~/.orca-colab folder removes everything the app stored.

Protect yourself from phishing

When you sign in, your browser lands on an address starting with http://127.0.0.1:. That address is a one-time key to your Colab. Paste it only into orca-colab running on your own machine; never send it to anyone or paste it into a website or chat.

Changes and contact

Changes to this policy are published on this page and in the source repository. Questions: open a GitHub issue.
